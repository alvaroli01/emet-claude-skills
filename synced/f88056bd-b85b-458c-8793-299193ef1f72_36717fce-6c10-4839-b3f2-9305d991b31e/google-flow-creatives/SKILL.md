# Google Flow / Veo 3 creatives that sell

Golden rule: the tool does not sell, the angle does. Decide who you are talking to and through which door BEFORE writing any prompt. The prompt is the last step.

Goal set by Alvaro: learn his taste until creatives come out right with **a single prompt**. Always read the "Alvaro's taste log" section at the end and apply every entry.

## When Alvaro sends a product photo (always do this first)
1. Look at the photo and describe the product in one line (type, colour, fabric, fit, men/women) so he can confirm you read it right.
2. **Always ask which angle he is looking for** before writing prompts. Use AskUserQuestion with the angles (Dolor, Deseo, Identidad, Precio-valor, Urgencia, Prueba social) and whether he wants **vídeo, foto o ambos**.
3. In the same question, **give your own recommendation** as the first option, marked "(Recomendado)": the angle you think works best for this product and why, focused on the **emotion** it triggers (e.g. confidence walking into an important meeting, feeling seen at a family dinner, the relief of finally having something to wear, the pride of a compliment from a stranger).
   - Base it on the product, audience temperature (cold traffic → Dolor or Deseo first) and the season.
   - Include a concrete scene idea (Spanish setting, ordinary person, what happens in second 1) for both video and photo.
4. Once he answers, write the prompts:
   - **Vídeo**: 3 prompts, same angle, 3 different openings (8 blocks each, video template below).
   - **Foto**: 3 prompts for Flow images, same angle, different scenes or framings (photo template below).
5. After he generates, ask what he liked and what failed, and update the taste log (learning loop).

## Alvaro's fixed style rules (always)
- **Location: it must be obvious it is Spain.** Allowed settings:
  - A Spanish home: bedroom, kitchen, living room, balcony, building entrance hall (portal) in a typical Spanish flat (tiled floors, wooden shutters/persianas, iron balcony railings).
  - A Spanish street: Madrid, Sevilla, Valencia or Barcelona-style streets, plazas, terrace bars, recognisable Spanish architecture, Spanish shop signs and street details.
  - **Alvaro's own store (Salvia Madrid)**: a boutique interior that clearly reads as Spain (Spanish signage, Madrid street visible through the window, warm Mediterranean light, sage-green brand touches). Never a generic mall or a non-Spanish shop.
  - Never studios, never places that could be anywhere.
- **People: normal-looking, everyday people. Never supermodels.** Realistic bodies, faces and skin, ordinary hair and makeup, real ages (20s–50s). They should look like a customer, not a model.
- **Format: UGC always.** Handheld phone-style camera, imperfect framing, natural light, real clutter, zero studio look.
- Store: Salvia Madrid (men's and women's fashion, Spain).

## What works in fashion ads (Alvaro's playbook)
In fashion, natural wins: less production, more authenticity, focus on the product.

**Videos that work**
- Natural and authentic, UGC style.
- Show clear **shots of the product** and create **contrast with everyday life** (the garment in a normal day: going out, commuting, at home, with friends).
- Low production: a natural video = more credibility.

**Images that work**
- **Good light**, natural and simple.
- Prioritise **product shots**, simple and calm.
- Create **focus and context** around the product (the garment is the hero, the setting explains when you'd wear it).

**Videos that do NOT work in fashion**
- Too perfect: film or TV-scene style.
- Over-produced models or scenes.
- Big, expensive productions that kill the naturalness.

Apply it to every prompt: never ask for cinematic, editorial, glossy or commercial looks; always ask for phone footage, natural light, everyday moments, and keep the product clearly visible in frame.

## Veo 3 and Flow basics
- Veo 3 is the model; Flow is the workspace (upload references, chain clips, build the scene). Use Flow, not the Gemini app, for real work. Flow runs in Alvaro's Chrome (Ultra plan); the chat assistant in the side panel also generates images.
- Generates video and audio together (ambience, sounds, lip-synced dialogue). Clips are about 8 seconds; longer ads are chained clips. Veo 3.1 adds vertical format, scene extension and 1080p/4K.
- Accepts a reference image: this is the key. With a real product photo it keeps the product and only generates movement, light and setting.
- Where it breaks: hands and fingers in close-up; on-screen text (always add text later in editing); fine detail such as prints, logos and engravings; continuity between clips (fix clothing, hair and light word for word); physics (objects appearing or passing through things).
- Use AI for: atmosphere, context, avatars talking to camera, B-roll, scroll-stopping openings. Do not use AI for the shot where the customer decides to buy: that needs the real product (real photo or real footage).

## Step 1: the avatar (one video = one avatar)
Not "woman 30–55" (that is targeting). An avatar answers 4 layers:
1. **Situation**: what they are doing when they see the ad, where, at what time, what is on their mind.
2. **Pain**: what bothers them today, in their words.
3. **Desire**: how they want to feel or look after buying.
4. **Objection**: what stops them (price, size, distrust, partner, shipping).
If you cannot answer all 4, do not write the prompt. Get real material from: competitor ad comments in the Ad Library, 3–4 star reviews of similar products, customer DMs and questions.

Example avatars for one garment:
| Avatar | Pain | Objection | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| La que se arregla | Bored of her wardrobe, does not want to spend much | "Seguro que la calidad es mala" | Desire |
| La que se esconde | Does not feel good in her clothes, avoids photos | "A mí eso no me queda" | Identity |
| La que regala | Birthday coming, does not know what to buy | "No llega a tiempo" | Urgency |
Never mix avatars or benefits in one video ("fits well, cheap, fast, quality" = no edge). One avatar, hammer it for 8 seconds.

## Step 2: the 6 angles (one creative = one angle)
1. **Pain (the problem)**: open on the problem, product appears at the end. E.g. someone in front of an open wardrobe, overwhelmed.
2. **Desire (the after)**: show life with the product, no problem. E.g. she walks out of the portal dressed up, late-afternoon light. Sell the scene, not the garment.
3. **Identity (who you are)**: "Esto es para las que...". Avatar talking to camera about who it is and is not for.
4. **Price and value (comparison)**: your offer next to the expensive alternative. Say "lo mismo por menos", never "barato". Split shot, two options, camera moves to yours.
5. **Urgency (the clock)**: only with a REAL reason (stock, delivery date, season). Fake urgency burns the brand. Fast rhythm, hard cuts.
6. **Social proof (others)**: converts best with warm audiences and new brands. Testimonial format, handheld, normal home. ONLY with a real review you can show (see honesty rules).

## Step 3: the matrix (angle by audience temperature)
| Angle | Audience | When | Opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | Cold | Nobody knows you, need to stop the scroll | Problem scene |
| Desire | Cold | Visual product that sells through the eyes | Beautiful result |
| Identity | Cold or warm | Clear niche with a community | Direct line to camera |
| Price-value | Warm | They know the category and compare | Two options at once |
| Urgency | Hot | Retargeting, abandoned cart, end of stock | Fast movement |
| Social proof | Warm or hot | New brand or doubts about quality | Face talking |
Always start with the first two rows: cold traffic is where the volume is.

Before the prompt (on paper, not in Flow): 1) pick the avatar, 2) pick the angle from the table, 3) write in one sentence what happens in second 1 (if unclear, the video is dead), 4) only now write the prompt.

## Step 4: the video prompt (8 blocks, in this order)
Prompt in **English**, dialogue in **Spanish from Spain** in quotes, with the accent stated (otherwise it comes out neutral Latin American).
0. **Reference** (always first, never removed):
   `Use the exact product from the attached reference image: same colour, same fabric, same cut, same details. Do not redesign, replace or restyle it. It must stay identical in every frame.`
1. **Subject**: who, in detail: age, hair, exact clothing, and always an ordinary-looking person (e.g. "an ordinary-looking woman in her mid 30s, average build, natural hair, minimal makeup, not a model").
2. **Action**: one clear verb. Never three actions in a row.
3. **Scene**: Spanish home, Spanish street or Salvia Madrid store, with at least two concrete details that prove it is Spain (e.g. wooden persianas, iron balconies, Spanish street signs, terrace bar with cañas).
4. **Camera**: shot type and movement (UGC: handheld, vertical, slight shake, imperfect framing as if a friend is filming).
5. **Light**: natural window light, golden hour, harsh midday, warm interior.
6. **Aesthetic**: kills the AI look: `Shot on a phone camera / 35mm, natural skin texture with visible pores, not airbrushed, not plastic, avoid AI render appearance, no on-screen text.`
7. **Audio**: ambience + dialogue: `She says in Spanish from Spain: "..."`

Video template:
```
Use the exact product from the attached reference image: same colour, same fabric, same cut, same details. Do not redesign, replace or restyle it. It must stay identical in every frame.
[SUBJECT] An ordinary-looking woman in her early 30s, average build, dark wavy hair, minimal makeup, not a model, wearing the product from the reference image.
[ACTION] She walks slowly toward the camera and smiles at the last second.
[SCENE] Narrow street in the centre of Madrid, balconies with iron railings, wooden shutters, Spanish street sign on the wall, late afternoon, people in the background.
[CAMERA] Vertical 9:16, medium shot, handheld phone footage, slight natural shake, subject centred.
[LIGHT] Golden hour, warm light from the left.
[AESTHETIC] Shot on a phone camera, natural skin texture with visible pores, not airbrushed, not plastic, avoid AI render appearance, no on-screen text.
[AUDIO] Ambient street sounds. She says in Spanish from Spain: "Llevo tres días seguidos poniéndomelo."
```

## Photo prompt (Flow images)
Same rules, no audio: one frozen moment that carries the emotion. Blocks: Reference · Subject · Moment (emotion caught mid-action) · Scene (obviously Spain) · Framing (4:5 for feed, 9:16 for stories; candid phone photo) · Light · Aesthetic.
```
Use the exact product from the attached reference image: same colour, same fabric, same cut, same details. Do not redesign, replace or restyle it.
[SUBJECT] An ordinary-looking man in his mid 30s, average build, short dark hair, light stubble, not a model, wearing the product from the reference image.
[MOMENT] Laughing naturally mid-conversation with a friend, caught off guard.
[SCENE] Terrace bar on a Sevilla street, metal tables, cañas on the table, orange trees and whitewashed walls behind.
[FRAMING] Vertical 4:5, candid phone photo taken by a friend across the table, slightly off-centre.
[LIGHT] Warm late-afternoon sun, soft shadows.
[AESTHETIC] Realistic smartphone photo, natural skin texture with visible pores, not airbrushed, avoid AI render appearance, no text, no logos.
```

### Example scene ideas
- Pain · bedroom: open wardrobe, two hangers, sighs "Otra vez lo mismo. Nada me sirve." Soft grey light through wooden persianas, unmade bed.
- Desire · street: walks out of a portal into a Spanish street, fixes her hair once, does not look at the camera, tracking shot backwards, golden hour. No dialogue. (Not looking at camera = does not read as an ad; works with sound off.)
- Identity · kitchen: late 20s on a stool with a coffee, talking to camera: "Esto no es para todo el mundo. Es para las que se cansaron de devolver." Real Spanish kitchen with dishes out.
- Social proof · portal: opening a delivery box in the building entrance with mailboxes, smiling naturally (dialogue only from a real customer review).
- Store · Salvia Madrid: ordinary customer trying a jacket in front of the mirror, Madrid street visible through the shop window, handheld, warm light.
- Men: same structure (e.g. ordinary man in his 30s on a Sevilla street or at a Madrid terrace bar).

## Generating and testing
- Always generate **3–4 variants of the same prompt**; the first is rarely the one. Do not rewrite the whole prompt when the problem was the roll.
- Test: 3 videos, same avatar, 3 different angles, same budget. The one that holds is the winning angle for that audience.
- Then keep the angle and make 5 versions changing only the opening. Never change two things at once.
- Warning sign: good retention in the first seconds but no clicks → the angle does not connect. Change the door, not the shot.
- Every variant must open differently, or they compete with each other.
- If the idea fits in 8 seconds, keep it at 8.

## Collection creatives (after a product wins)
When a single product is scaled to 100 €/day and Alvaro builds a collection around it (see ecom-profit-check):
- **10 creatives: 7 videos + 3 images.**
- Iterate on the winning concept of the product campaign: same angle, same style, same concept.
- If the winner was UGC, keep **the same person** (identical subject description, word for word, in every prompt) and the same style.
- Product photos for the collection: **same background and same person across every product**, so the collection looks consistent.
- Still vary the openings between the 10 creatives so they do not compete with each other.

## Creative refresh batches (scaled campaigns)
| Campaign | From 200 €/day | From 500 €/day |
|---|---|---|
| Collection | 10 new creatives every 2 days | 10 new creatives every day |
| Single product | 5 new creatives every 2 days | 5 new creatives every day |
- Also a new batch every time the budget doubles (100 → 200 → 400 → 800 €).
- **70 / 30 split**: 70% iterations of the current winners (same concept, new opening, new scene or new person), 30% a completely new concept (new angle or new avatar).
- Always launched in a **new ad set inside the same campaign**.

## 90-minute session routine
| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0–20 min | Avatar (4 layers) and angle. Flow closed. |
| 20–35 min | Write 3 prompts with the 8 blocks: same angle, 3 different openings. |
| 35–70 min | Generate 3–4 rolls per prompt; discard fast. |
| 70–90 min | Edit, add text and subtitles outside Flow, export vertical, label as AI. |
Leave with 3 ready creatives of the same angle, not one perfect one.

## Checklists
Before generating: avatar written with 4 layers · ONE angle chosen and why · second 1 defined · real product reference image ready · prompt has the reference sentence and all 8 blocks · no on-screen text + real skin texture requested · obviously Spanish location + ordinary-looking person + UGC style · low production, product clearly visible · every entry in the taste log applied.

After generating (5 filters): is the product mine or did it invent something? · do hands, fingers and face hold up full screen? · does it make sense with the sound off? · is the opening different from my other variants? · is it labelled as AI-generated content? Plus: would someone instantly know this is Spain? Does it look natural, not like a TV ad?

## The 7 mistakes
1. Starting with the prompt instead of avatar and angle. 2. Vague prompt ("pretty girl in a nice dress"). 3. No reference image. 4. All variants open the same. 5. Text inside the video (add it in editing). 6. Keeping the first roll. 7. Long video for the sake of it.

## Honesty rules (non-negotiable)
- A generated testimonial is not a real testimonial. An AI person giving an invented opinion is misleading advertising and can get the ad or account removed. Social-proof videos may only dramatise a real written review you can show, or your own brand message. If nobody really said the sentence, do not put it in anyone's mouth.
- Label AI-generated content on the platforms (Meta requires it and detects it).
- Urgency only with a true reason.
- Never reproduce another brand's logo, product or trademark.

## Learning loop (aim: perfect in one prompt)
1. After each Flow session, ask Alvaro which result he liked most and what was wrong with the rest (person, place, light, camera, audio, product accuracy).
2. Turn every correction into a concrete prompt rule (e.g. "too polished" → add "no makeup, messy hair"; "doesn't look like Spain" → add specific Spanish details).
3. Save the full prompt of every approved creative as a "winning prompt" example.
4. Offer to add these to the taste log below with a skill update, so the next first prompt already includes them.
5. Track which prompts needed only 1 roll: those are the model to copy.
